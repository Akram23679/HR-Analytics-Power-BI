01 / project brief

Understand the workforce, then identify where HR can act.

The project examines employee demographics, hiring trends, work location, departmental distribution and attrition. The goal is to translate workforce records into evidence that can support retention, workforce planning and people-related decisions.

02 / data and method

Prepare employee records before measuring workforce trends.

The analysis uses the HR dataset and applies consistent date handling and employee-status logic. The report uses 29 September 2026 as its analysis cut-off for term-date interpretation.

Data preparationQuality checks
  • Loaded and reviewed 22,214 employee records.
  • Checked employee IDs for duplicates.
  • Converted hire and birth dates using day-month-year locale.
  • Handled blank term dates as active employees.
  • Treated future-dated term dates as active at the project cut-off.
  • Created Employee Status, Age Group, Hire Year, Term Year, Tenure and Tenure Group fields in DAX.
Tools and techniquesStack
  • MySQL Workbench: database access.
  • Microsoft Excel: data review and preparation.
  • Power BI Desktop: two-page workforce and attrition dashboard.
  • Tableau: parallel dashboard and calculated fields.
  • DAX: employee status, age, tenure and date attributes.

04 / findings

Where the workforce data signals an opportunity.

These findings summarize patterns reported in the HR Analytics project. They describe associations in the dataset and should not be interpreted as proof of cause.

≈1,100

Consistent annual hiring

Annual hiring stayed broadly steady from 2001 to 2020, with no major hiring freeze or sudden expansion evident in the report.

81%

Ohio workforce concentration

18,025 employees are based in Ohio, with 16,871 located in Cleveland. The workforce is highly concentrated around headquarters.

50.8 / 46.5%

Near-balanced gender mix

Male employees account for 50.8%, female employees 46.5%, and non-conforming employees 2.7%.

39%

Early-tenure retention

1,143 of 2,901 recorded leavers (39%) left within their first five years.

19.2%

Auditing has the highest rate

Auditing has the highest reported attrition rate, but the department has only 52 employees. Legal is a larger-team warning signal at 16.7%.

12.3% vs 13.3%

Remote vs headquarters

Remote employees have a lower reported attrition rate than headquarters employees in this dataset. This is an association, not proof that remote work causes lower attrition.

05 / recommendations

Eight actions to strengthen retention and workforce planning.

The recommendations follow the opportunities identified in the project report, with an emphasis on early-tenure retention, targeted investigation and data quality.

01

Strengthen early-career retention

Improve onboarding, assign mentors, schedule 6-month / 1-year / 3-year check-ins, and clarify career paths and promotion criteria.

02

Investigate Legal and Auditing

Use exit interviews and engagement surveys to examine workload, compensation and growth opportunities. Interpret Auditing carefully because its team is small.

03

Plan hiring against attrition

Track quarterly net headcount (hires minus leavers) and adjust hiring targets as employee departures change.

04

Support flexible work

Continue evaluating remote and hybrid options as a potential retention lever, while monitoring outcomes by department.

05

Prepare for succession

About one-fifth of the workforce is aged 50 or older. Plan succession and knowledge transfer for critical roles.

06

Reduce location concentration

Consider growing branch offices or remote hiring to broaden the talent pool and reduce dependence on Ohio.

07

Maintain diversity monitoring

Continue reviewing gender and race representation and compare attrition across groups over time.

08

Improve HR data quality

Review future-dated terminations, add more recent hires, and calculate age automatically from birthdate.

06 / limitations and next steps

Interpret the results within the dataset’s limits.

The project report identifies data coverage and date-handling considerations that should be addressed before using the metrics for operational decisions.

Limitations
  • The dataset is a snapshot and does not by itself explain why employees leave.
  • Future-dated termination records were treated as active at the 29 September 2026 cut-off.
  • Some department-level rates are based on small employee populations; interpret them with counts.
  • Demographic and location comparisons show patterns, not causation.
Suggested next steps
  • Validate the term-date cut-off and refresh the dataset regularly.
  • Combine attrition metrics with exit interviews and engagement feedback.
  • Track early-tenure retention cohorts over time.
  • Monitor quarterly hiring, exits and net headcount by department and location.
